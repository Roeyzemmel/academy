## Statement

> **Remark 1.3 (hyperellipticity — what (A2) does and does not say).**
> If $M$ lies in a hyperelliptic component then (A2) holds: the hyperelliptic
> involution $J$ satisfies $J^*\omega = -\omega$, is affine with derivative $-I$,
> and is an involution. **The converse is false, and (A2) does not assume the
> involution is hyperelliptic** — it assumes only that *some* affine involution
> with derivative $-I$ exists. In higher genus the involution is typically not
> hyperelliptic.
>
> Since $\mathcal{H}(2)$ and $\mathcal{H}(1,1)$ coincide with their hyperelliptic
> components (Kontsevich–Zorich), (A2) — hence (A1) — carries no information in
> genus 2. Whatever work the chain does above that must be done by $\Phi$ or by the
> unfolding structure.
>
> **Roey's observation, recorded and Not verified here:** under (SC) — $M$ the
> unfolding of a *simply connected* parking garage — the hyperelliptic case is
> interesting in its own right and worth separating. Consistent with the two cases
> we have: the rectangle (a torus) and the L-shaped table ($\mathcal{H}(2)$, genus 2,
> necessarily hyperelliptic). What would need checking is that $\iota$ has $2g+2$
> fixed points on $M$, equivalently that $M/\langle\iota\rangle$ has genus 0.
