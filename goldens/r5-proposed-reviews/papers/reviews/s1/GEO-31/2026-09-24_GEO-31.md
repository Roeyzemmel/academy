GEO-31 findings, both runs:
- **Semi-corner sense (both runs, OVERSTATED → WEAKEN_CLAIM).** BilliardIllumination
  `defn:billiard-pathologies` (flat_structures.tex:1185) allows $k\ge1$, and a *marked* boundary
  point of angle $\pi$ counts as a semi-corner (roadmap 13(k); arithmetic.tex:217–219). This
  database marks every square corner, so in the paper's sense every polyomino with a side of
  length $\ge2$ has $k=1$ semi-corners. Both runs counted them: 2×1 has 2, 2×3 has 6, L3, T4
  and S4 have 2, P5 has 4; only 1×1 and X5 have 0. The statement is true for corners =
  boundary points of angle $\ne\pi$ (PA-5's own definition). The same gloss in
  `assumptions/PA-5.md` needs the same restriction.
- **The PA-4 conjunct of PA-5 (both runs, UNJUSTIFIED → ADD_DERIVATION).** It is not written.
  It holds by definition: an embedded $P$ has a flat interior, so it has no interior cone
  points, and $h$ is the inclusion. Equivalently it is the recorded edge PA-6 ⇒ PA-5w ⇒ PA-4.
- **"Below $2\pi$" (both runs, UNJUSTIFIED → ADD_DERIVATION).** $h$ is injective, so a sector
  of angle $>2\pi$ overlaps itself, and angle $2\pi$ is a slit tip whose two sides coincide.
- **"Simple" is never used (both runs, UNDERSTATED).** The argument gives PA-5 for any
  rectangle-tiled region embedded in the plane, holes included; the 3×3 frame gives
  $[1^4,3^4]$ (run A) and 4 convex + 4 reflex corners (run B). GEO-32's frame example needs
  this form. "Polyomino" drifts between the two items: PA-6 says simple, GEO-32 uses the
  word for a region with a hole.
- **"Embedded" does the work (both runs).** Run A: a slit square gives $m=4$ and a helicoidal
  ramp gives $m=6$, each an immersed PA-4 garage failing PA-5. Run B: six squares around one
  vertex give an immersed disc with a $3\pi$ corner; it satisfies PA-4, PA-SC and PA-5w but
  not PA-5.
- **Definition (run B, no effect here).** If "turning point" meant a change of direction,
  angles $3\pi,5\pi,\dots$ would not count as turning points and PA-5 would not forbid them. So
  "corner" should read "boundary point of angle $\ne\pi$".
- **Checks.** Named examples only (1×1, 2×1, 2×3, 5×3, L3, L4, T4, S4, X5, U5, W5, P5, the frame,
  and pinched and immersed variants). The commutator cycle types match GEO-9/GEO-16, and
  #convex − #reflex = 4. Both runs did an exhaustive 16-configuration local check at a grid
  vertex. Scripts: scratchpad `geo31_check.py` (A), `geo31.py` and `spiral.py` (B).

Decision: **Proved / Proved → GEO-31, Proved**, with the wording repairs below applied at
transcription.
Allowed wording: "Let $P$ be a rectangle-tiled polygon **embedded** in the plane (in particular
any PA-6 witness). Every corner of $P$ — every boundary point of angle $\ne\pi$ — has angle
$\pi/2$ or $3\pi/2$. Hence PA-6 ⇒ PA-5 (the PA-4 conjunct: an embedded $P$ has flat interior
and $h$ is the inclusion; equivalently PA-6 ⇒ PA-5w ⇒ PA-4). The proof uses only
embeddedness and axis-parallel sides, not simple connectivity, so it also covers embedded
regions with holes." Do not say "no semi-corner" without the qualifier "in PA-5's sense
(no corner of angle $k\pi$, $k\ge2$)". In BilliardIllumination's sense, marked points of
angle $\pi$ are semi-corners.
Open: the paper's definition is a sketch pending Roey (k = 1 reading); whether PA-5 should
also exclude marked angle-$\pi$ points to match the paper is Roey's call. The edge
PA-6 ⇒ PA-5 moves from `implies_pending` to `implies`.
