# Traps: translation surfaces, billiards, illumination

The mistakes that actually recur in this area, collected in one place. Every trap points
to the theorem-sheet entry (`theorems/…`) or API recipe (`computation/api/…`) that
carries the precise statement; read that entry before relying on the one-line version
here. Graders use this file with the base plugin's `rigor` checklist (proof review) or
with the experiment-review checklist (computation review).

## A. Mathematical traps (the four that cost the most)

### A1. "Torus cover" ≠ "square-tiled" ≠ "arithmetic"

In Lelièvre–Monteil–Weiss a torus cover may branch over *several* points; equivalently
the group generated by *absolute* period holonomies is discrete. Relative periods can be
irrational, so such a surface need not be square-tiled and need not even be a lattice
surface. LMW's blocking dichotomy is about this weaker class. Swapping in
"square-tiled" inverts the content of the theorem.
→ `theorems/illumination.md` §1.0 (the trap box), §1.1; `theorems/veech.md` §3.4.

### A2. Finiteness is per source point, not for pairs

"Everything is illuminated" says: for each fixed $x$, the set of $y$ not illuminated
by $x$ is finite. The set of unilluminated *pairs* in $M \times M$ can be infinite —
Hubert–Schmoll–Troubetzkoy built a non-torus-cover with infinitely many such pairs, and
LMW's "moreover" clause (finitely many embedded surfaces $M' \subset M\times M$) is
precisely what allows it. Finiteness of pairs is a theorem about *rational polygons*
(Wolecki), not about translation surfaces.
→ `theorems/illumination.md` §1.2, §1.4.

### A3. Marked points change the stratum and are never harmless

A marked point is a zero of order 0; adding one moves $\mathcal{H}(2)$ to
$\mathcal{H}(2,0)$, changes the dimension, and changes what "the orbit closure" means.
Much of the Apisa–Wright illumination machinery is literally a theory of marked points,
so in this area the bookkeeping is the mathematics. When unfolding a billiard, check
whether the construction produced marked points or a cover rather than the primitive
surface.
→ `theorems/illumination.md` §1.9–§1.11; `theorems/strata.md` §4.1.

### A4. Illumination is the $\mathrm{bc}=0$ case of finite blocking

Two points fail to illuminate each other exactly when there is *no* straight-line path
between them — the empty-blocking-set case. Results about finite blocking with
$\mathrm{bc} \ge 1$ do not transfer automatically, and vice versa.
→ `theorems/illumination.md` §1.0.

## B. Traps in the conduct of a proof or a search

These are the domain instances of generic rules in `academy:rigor` and
`academy:citation-discipline`. The quoted sentences are the original wording of the old
`math-proof-writing` skill.

### B1. Specify the counterexample before hunting for it

"Concretely: which surface, which genus, what would have to go wrong, what the smallest
case is." Name the stratum (with its marked points, A3), the genus and the smallest
candidate surface first; the dimension count $\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal{H}(k_1,\dots,k_n)
= 2g+n-1$ (`theorems/strata.md` §4.1) is the cheapest first filter.

### B2. A blocked route is not a blocked question

"\"Tokarsky's construction doesn't transfer, because those corners unfold to regular
points\" is a correct observation about one route. It is not evidence that no
counterexample exists, and treating it as such is how a findable example gets written up
as an open question." When Tokarsky's construction (`theorems/illumination.md` §1.5)
fails to transfer, record that as a fact about the construction, never as evidence for
illumination.

### B3. Name what the searched class structurally excludes

A search reported as "no counterexample among origamis with ≤ 6 squares, searching over
vertex pairs only" says nothing outside that class. What it cannot see, from the sheet:
- **every non-square-tiled surface**: origamis are square-tiled, hence torus covers in
  the LMW sense, and torus covers are exactly the surfaces with the blocking property
  (`theorems/illumination.md` §1.0–§1.1). A search over origamis therefore never meets
  the non-torus-cover behaviour of §1.2 (infinitely many unilluminated pairs, HST) or
  the double pentagon of `examples.md`;
- **every pair that is not a vertex pair**: regular points, including periodic points
  (§1.9, §1.14), which the marked-point results single out.

Put the excluded class in the report (`computation/api/practice.md` §9 has the
computational side).

### B4. Check the ambient hypotheses before invoking the orbit-closure theorems

"\"By [EM18], the orbit closure is an affine invariant submanifold\" is only legitimate
once it is said why the ambient hypotheses hold." Eskin–Mirzakhani is about ergodic
$P$-invariant *probability* measures on $\mathcal{H}_1(\alpha)$; EMM is about orbits of
a point of $\mathcal{H}_1(\alpha)$ (`theorems/orbit-closures.md` §2.1, §2.2, and their
"Common misuse" lines). Say which stratum, with which marked points, before citing.

### B5. Primitivity is a silent hypothesis

"[gap: this step needs the surface to be primitive — not yet checked]". Several
results hold only for primitive surfaces (no translation map to a surface of lower
genus): Apisa's genus-two blocking theorem and Möller's list of periodic points are
stated for **primitive genus two** (`theorems/illumination.md` §1.13, §1.14; the
definition is quoted in §1.6); the Veech-group covering lemma needs a primitive base
(`theorems/veech.md` §3.3). Unfolding often yields a cover rather than the primitive
surface (A3), so primitivity has to be checked, not assumed.

### B6. Degenerate cases

`academy:rigor`'s degenerate-case list dropped "genus zero" for neutrality. In this
domain it belongs back on the list, on the polygon side of the billiard dictionary
(a polygon is not itself a translation surface; its unfolding is). *(Placement is a
judgement call of the 2026-09-28 pack build, not a sourced statement.)* The square torus
(`examples.md`) is the degenerate case on the surface side.

## C. API traps (sage-flatsurf 0.8.0, surface_dynamics 0.7.0)

"These are the ones that cost an afternoon each" (old `flatsurf-computation` skill). The
ranked full list is `computation/api/practice.md` §9 and `computation/api/quick-reference.md`.

- `saddle_connections(B)` bounds the **squared** length — the keyword is literally
  `squared_length_bound`.
- `SaddleConnection` has `start_data()` / `end_data()` returning `(label, vertex)`.
  There is no `start()` / `end()`, despite what older snippets suggest.
- There is **no** `surface.cylinder_decomposition(direction)`; go through
  `GL2ROrbitClosure.decomposition(v, limit)`.
- libflatsurf's `cyl.area()` returns **twice** the area. Miss this and every
  modulus is wrong by a factor of 2 — and wrong by a consistent factor is the
  hardest kind of wrong to notice.
- sage-flatsurf's `veech_group()` raises `NotImplementedError` for generators. A
  real Veech group needs a square-tiled surface via `surface_dynamics`.
- `t.cusps()` does not exist on a Teichmüller curve; it is `cusp_representatives()`
  returning `(origami, width)` pairs.
- Deprecated and to be avoided: `delaunay_decomposition()`,
  `delaunay_triangulation()`, `Singularity()`, and `Stratum.zeros/genus/nb_zeros`.
- `Origami` is **1-based** in its string / permutation form and **0-based** in
  `r_tuple()` / `u_tuple()` (`computation/api/origamis.md` §12.5).
- `import pyflatsurf` (and so `canonicalize()`, `GL2ROrbitClosure`) segfaults in an env
  reached by `PATH` only; it needs a full `conda activate`, and gcc/gxx at 14
  (`computation/api/setup.md` §12.4).
- Plausible deep imports such as `from sage.libs.gap.libgap import libgap` fail as the
  first Sage import under plain `python`; import `sage.all` first
  (`computation/api/libgap.md` §12.2).

And the rule behind all of them: when an experiment produces something surprising,
suspect the code first — a stratum mismatch, a squared-vs-linear bound, the factor of
2 — before believing a new theorem.
