## 6. UNVERIFIED — check before citing

Everything in this section is either a numbering detail, an attribution, or a claim I could not
confirm against a primary source. Do not put any of it into a paper without re-reading the source.

1. **Wright, Cylinder Deformation Theorem — the number of the refined statement.** Reported as
   "Theorem 5.1" from a machine extraction of the author's posted PDF. The introduction version is
   definitely Theorem 1.1. The refined ($\mathcal{M}$-parallel equivalence class) version's number
   in the printed Geom. Topol. **19** (2015) 413–438 was **not** confirmed. Safest citation:
   "Wright's Cylinder Deformation Theorem [Wri15]".

2. **Mirzakhani–Wright boundary paper — theorem numbers.** The abstract is verbatim from Springer.
   The intro theorem/corollary numbers (1.1, 1.2, 1.3) and their exact phrasings were extracted
   mechanically and were internally inconsistent across fetches (one fetch attributed the full-rank
   classification to this paper, which is actually the separate Duke 2018 paper). **Re-read the
   Inventiones version.**

3. **Eskin–Filip–Wright Theorem 1.5** (finiteness; "rank 1 and degree at most 2", "rank 2 degree 1"
   collections). Extracted mechanically; the meaning of "degree" here and the exact quantifiers
   were not independently confirmed.

4. **Eskin–Filip–Wright and periodic points.** Apisa–Wright's Theorem 1.2 is attributed to EFW via
   "Section 4.2 explains why Theorem 1.2 is a special case of results in [EFW]". No theorem stated
   in terms of periodic points was located in EFW itself. Cite through Apisa–Wright.

5. **Wolecki's Lemma 3.1** (Tokarsky's lemma, generalized). The reported hypotheses — "angle
   $\pi/n$ at $A$, angle $m\pi/n$ at $B$, $n$ even, $m<n-1$ a positive integer" — come from a
   single machine extraction and the conclusion ("no billiard trajectory in $Q$ from $A$ coming
   back to $A$") reads as a self-illumination statement. Verify directly.

6. **Tokarsky (1995).** The Monthly article itself was **not** read. The bibliographic data
   (Amer. Math. Monthly 102 (1995), no. 10, 867–879), the "26 sides", the claim of a later 4-sided
   room with two *boundary* points, and Castro's 24-gon in *Quantum* (1997) all come from
   Wikipedia's illumination-problem article and Wolecki's historical remarks, not from the
   primary sources.

7. **Wolecki's publication status.** No journal version of arXiv:1905.09358 was found. It may still
   be unpublished. Check before citing as a journal article.

8. **"Balanced" translation covering.** The definition given ($p^{-1}(\Sigma(X)) = \Sigma(Y)$) and
   the subgroup lemma ($\Gamma(Y) \le \Gamma(X)$ for a finite balanced covering of a primitive base
   of genus $>1$) were quoted from a *secondary* source (arXiv:1005.4588), and that source
   attributes the surrounding framework to Möller (2006), not to Gutkin–Judge. The original
   Gutkin–Judge (Duke 103, 2000) formulation, and specifically whether they prove
   **commensurability** of the two Veech groups under a balanced covering, was **not verified**.
   The commonly cited "balanced covering $\Rightarrow$ commensurable Veech groups" statement is
   plausible but unconfirmed here.

9. **Kerckhoff–Masur–Smillie statement.** Standard formulation given; the printed theorem was not
   read (the Annals page has no abstract).

10. **Masur–Veech "and even mixing".** Quoted from a secondary source; the mixing statement is
    usually attributed to Veech (and later to Avila–Gouëzel–Yoccoz for exponential mixing). Not
    verified against Masur 1982 / Veech 1982 directly.

11. **Ineffectivity of EMM-based illumination bounds** (§5(c)). This is my inference from the
    method (EM/EMM proofs give no constants) and is universally understood in the field, but no
    source consulted states it explicitly for the illumination theorems.

12. **Apisa–Wright, Marked points — journal pagination.** Confirmed as "Geom. Topol. 2021" from
    Wright's own page; volume/pages not confirmed. Similarly Apisa's marked-strata paper is
    "Geom. Topol. 2020" with volume/pages unconfirmed, and Apisa's genus-two paper is listed as
    "IMRN, to appear."

13. **HST §8 example.** LMW state verbatim that "Hubert, Schmoll and Troubetzkoy [HST08] have
    constructed an example of a translation surface $M$ which is not a torus cover, and in which
    there are infinitely many pairs of points which do not illuminate each other." Attempts to
    locate that construction directly in the arXiv HTML of math/0602394 failed (fetches returned
    only Theorems 1/2 and the Questions section). The claim is well-sourced via LMW and Wolecki,
    but the construction itself was not read.

14. **Wright's survey (arXiv:1411.1827) and Bull. AMS article.** Both were targeted as clean
    sources for the Veech dichotomy and EM/EMM statements but could not be fetched past their
    opening sections. The statements in §3.1 and §2.1–2.2 therefore come from other sources
    (Hubert–Schmidt, Cheung–Hubert–Masur, the Annals/IHÉS papers themselves).
