## Statement

### N6 (Proved) — a correction to R §5.3: $K_{\min}=3$ for the $2\times1$ rectangle

**N6.** The table in `notes/03-q2/normal-case.md` under [R §5.3] lists the $2\times1$
rectangle, $G=\mathbb Z/4\times\mathbb Z/2$, with the three shortest directions $x$, $y$,
$xy$ giving $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(1,1)$, and marks the covering as succeeding. That is wrong.

Writing $G$ additively, the union of the cyclic subgroups generated by $(1,0)$, $(0,1)$ and
$(1,1)$ is
$$\{(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(3,0),(0,1),(1,1),(3,1)\},$$
which omits $(2,1)$. **Coprimality of $(p,q)$ is not used anywhere in this argument** — it
holds for every integer pair, and the argument is quadrant-free, so it in fact covers every
direction of size at most $2$, not merely the three words $x,y,xy$ that the table lists. The
value of a Christoffel word of direction $(p,q)$ is $(p\bmod 4,\;q\bmod 2)$ **because $G$ is
abelian**, which is what makes the word-order convention immaterial here; its $m$-th power is
$(mp\bmod 4,\;mq\bmod 2)$. So reaching $(2,1)$ needs $m$ odd and $q$ odd, hence
$p\equiv2\pmod 4$, first achieved by the direction $(2,1)$, of size $3$, via the word $xxy$.
Hence $K_{\min}=3$ for the $2\times1$ rectangle, not $2$. Passing from "$C^{\mathrm{pow}}$
covers $G\setminus\{1\}$" to (Q2) is R Prop. 5.7 (the normal case), or directly R Thm 5.1(b)
for a regular abelian action, restricted here to values of direction size at most $K$.

**Orientation.** $K_{\min}=3$ is orientation-independent, because direction size is
symmetric, but the winning word is not: R2 Prop. 7.1 writes the period lattice
$4\mathbb Z\oplus2\mathbb Z$ for the $1\times2^r$ rectangle, which under R Prop. 3.1's
convention is the *transpose* of the $2\times1$ orientation used above, and on that
transposed surface the winning word is $xyy$ (direction $(1,2)$), not $xxy$. A later
regression pinning the *word* fixes the orientation.

**Attribution.** R2 Prop. 7.1, recorded **Proved**, already carries *both* bounds — at least
$2^r+1$ for the $1\times2^r$ rectangle, and at most $p+q$ under (A7) for a $p\times q$
rectangle (the upper bound is stated there without proof, but is one line — representatives
within the rectangle — and both verifier runs below checked it). On this surface the two
bounds meet, at $3$ and $3$, so $K_{\min}=3$ already follows from that one Proved item. N6's
own contribution is not the number but the **refutation**: that the R §5.3 row's covering
claim at $K=2$ is false, and that the missing element is $(2,1)$, realised by $\sigma^2\tau$,
which pairs the squares $\{0,7\},\{1,6\},\{2,5\},\{3,4\}$ — a single $\Phi$-invariant
orbital. What was at fault on this row is R Conj. 5.8's own sentence "True with $K = 2$ in
all three examples above"; the fault is isolated to the $2\times1$ rectangle — the
Wollmilchsau's three shortest directions still cover the quaternion group and the $D_4$
origami's still cover $D_4$, both at $K=2$.

*Cleared by.* Two `claim-verifier` runs on the primary model (Fable 5.1), both returning
Proved/CONFIRMED on 2026-09-20, recorded in `computation/verdicts.md`. Both independently
rebuilt the eight-square surface and reproduced the same coverage of the 56 ordered
off-diagonal pairs — 32 at direction size 1, 48 at size 2, 56 at size 3 — by a complete hand
enumeration over the 8-element group, exhaustive at that size; the rebuild script each wrote
was not executed by either run, one for lack of a shell tool. Two earlier fallback-model runs
reached the same label and the same numbers independently but, being on the fallback model,
do not clear the claim; they are recorded as corroborating only.

*Consequence.* The regression suite pins $K_{\min}=3$ for the $2\times1$ rectangle, and a
pipeline returning $2$ is wrong.
